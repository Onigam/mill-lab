# 3D worlds

> **Polish pass of 2026-10-30.** The water and the scenery were redone after this note was written: for what the water looks like and reads from the state see `docs/water.md`, for the land, plants, buildings and sky see `docs/scenery.md`. Statements below about a "falling sheet", "sheets of water", "tiles of earth" or "70 trees" describe the first version and are superseded by those two notes; the structure, ids, animation mapping and tests described here still hold.

Four worlds built from `src/three/dims.ts` (metres, the only source of dimensions: every constant says `reported`, `derived`, `sim` or
`estimate`; whatever `src/sim` derives is imported, never retyped) with the frozen ids of `src/part-ids.ts`: `site` 12 parts, `screw` **17** parts, `generator` 8 parts and
`powerroom` 10 parts (the last two are described in "Completion wave A", below; the electronics left the `screw` world for the `powerroom` one).
`src/three/index.ts` exports `createSiteScene()`, `createScrewScene(opts?)`, `createGeneratorScene()` and `createPowerroomScene()`, all `lazyModule`s (nothing is built until the world is shown).
Checked by `*.test.ts` beside the code (see Tests). The screw is **generic**: sized by the published design rules at the lab's design point
(1.75 m, 180 L/s: outer diameter 0.86 m, inner 0.46 m, pitch 0.86 m, length 4.67 m, slope 22°, 3 threads, gap 4.2 mm), never drawn as the
association's machine; no brand, person or company name appears in any label.

## Frames

- **screw world, world frame**: X horizontal along the screw (the upper end and the drive on the +X side, the water flows towards -X), Y up, Z
  towards the viewer; origin on the floor under the lower end of the flights. The **screw frame** is the world frame tilted by the slope about Z
  and moved to A0 (the axis at the lower end of the flights): X' along the axis towards the upper end, Y' up, Z across. The section plane is z = 0.
- **thread**: RIGHT-HANDED (x' = x0 + pitch ψ / 2π, ψ from +Y' towards +Z). The turbine turns in the positive sense about +X' (`rotation.x = +angle`),
  which makes the pockets of water go down the slope. Both facts are tested on the geometry (`helix.test.ts`, `screw.test.ts`).
- **site frame**: X east, Y up, Z south (towards the visitor), datum Y = 0 the design level of the headwater; the river and the canal flow towards -X.
  The tailrace is **compressed**: about 21 m and the turn to the river, where the real one is about 100 m (S1 12:36); everything else is at true scale.

### What `update(state, dt, view)` reads

| State                                                           | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `angle` (rad, already slowed)                                   | unwrapped each frame; turns core, blades, fasteners, then every wheel by `angle × its ratio`; moves the pockets (one blade spacing per third of a turn), the caps of the blades in the section, the fish; scrolls the chain and belt patterns                                  |
| `omega`, `view.slowMo`                                          | visible rate of every wheel (the wagon-wheel guard, below) and the speed of the water dots (`pitch × rev/s`)                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `fill` (0..1)                                                   | the bucket is rebuilt when it moves by 0.012 (the level of a bucket is `hMin + fill (hPass - hMin)`), the polygon of the section too, the level of the basin (`fill` = 1 is the lowest saddle, less water lowers the pool, as the simulation's drawdown); no water under 0.015 |
| `tailRise`                                                      | top of the outlet pool = design tail level (`0.6 D cos β` over the lowest point of the trough) + `tailRise`; the pool is murkier when flooded                                                                                                                                  |
| `outerDiameter`, `innerDiameter`, `pitch`, `length`, `slopeDeg` | the world is **rebuilt** at that size when one changes by 0.4 % (at most every 0.15 s: about 15 to 25 ms of work, geometry only, the registered groups stay, `PartEntry.meshes` is refreshed)                                                                                  |
| `rpm`, `generatorRpm`                                           | without the sim's ratios (see below) the belt ratio is `generatorRpm / rpm` over the default chain ratio                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `current` (A)                                                   | speed of the dots of the current (0.03 m/s per ampere from the generator's terminal to the end of the platform, 0.022 on the cable out): the lamp, the bars and the meter are in the `powerroom` world                                                                         |
| `view.cutaway`, `view.schematic`                                | the section (below), the "Flux" dots: water first (down the pockets, the basin and the pool), then the current from the generator to the battery and out                                                                                                                       |
| zeros in the size (`machine` is a wheel)                        | **the screw is left drawn at rest**: no rotation, no water, no rebuild; nothing is drawn for the wheel (a comparison only, no part, no world)                                                                                                                                  |

**Wagon-wheel guard.** A wheel has `symmetry` identical positions per turn (teeth, holes, 1 for a plain disc). Above 14 positions a second a
translucent disc fades in (full at 24) and the wheel stops following the true angle: it moves at most 14 positions a second. The big sprocket, the
small sprocket, the two pulleys have a disc; the shafts and the generator stub are plain. The patterns of the chain and the belt never run faster than 12 repeats
a second. At the design point (55 rpm) the small sprocket (17 teeth, 3 rev/s) is blurred, the generator pulley (10 rev/s) too, the big sprocket and
the screw are not.

**Ratios.** `createScrewScene({ ratios: () => ({ chain, belt }) })` takes the live ratios (the lead passes `sim.params.chainRatio` and `.beltRatio`); without it the
chain ratio is the default of the simulation for that diameter (`defaultDriveRatios`, the arithmetic of `plantFor`) and the belt takes the rest of `generatorRpm / rpm`. A
change redraws the small sprocket (teeth = 56 / ratio), the small pulley (radius = large / ratio), the chain and the belt.

### The section (`view.cutaway`)

The plane is the vertical plane through the axis (z = 0); the half towards the viewer is removed from the materials of `trough`, `screw-core`,
`blade-module`, `blade-fastener`, `inlet-sill`, `outlet-pool` and `trapped-water` (and nothing of the drive, which stands beside the axis). Caps close
what is cut: the trough's bottom strip and the piers, the core tube and the shaft, **the blades as radial rectangles that follow the angle** (the helicoid is
ruled: its section by a plane through the axis is a straight radial line, so the staircase of blades above and below the axis is exact), the headwall, the slab and
the wall of the pool, and the water: the pocket's polygon (a rectangle between two blades while the bucket is full, a wedge under the level line
when it is not) and the rectangles of the two pools. A test requires that every pocket is bounded, at five angles, by the face of one blade on one side and the
face of the next on the other.

### The drive train in the site world (`src/three/site/drive.ts`)

At the top end of the screw the site draws **the same machine as the screw world**, connected and at its true size: the large sprocket on the screw's own shaft (the shaft goes on through the upper
bearing, 1.65 m past the end of the flights, as in the screw world), the chain, the small sprocket on the countershaft in its two pillow blocks, the large pulley, the belt, the small pulley on the generator's
shaft, the generator. The electronics are not drawn here (the powerhouse houses them; its sheet says so): the cable from the generator's terminal goes to the powerhouse's west wall, where the old line
arrived, and is redrawn when the screw is redrawn at another size (the dots of the current follow it).

- **Same builder, same numbers.** `buildDriveMachine` (`screw/build.ts`: `buildDriveParts` is now the machine plus the electronics, nothing else changed in the screw world) builds the eight parts in the
  screw's world frame; the site moves that frame by a pure translation, `siteFrame(l)` = (`xWest + lowerEnd`, the height that pins the pool to `SITE.tailY`, the middle of the canal). One group per part
  (`site-drive-sprocket`, …), unregistered children of `water-chamber`: the part's bounds, explode, isolate, ghost and X-ray act on them (kit-drivable `MeshStandardMaterial`s; the wagon-wheel discs are
  scenery, as in the screw world). A test compares the box of each part, translated back, with the screw world's box (3 mm), at the design ratios and at others; the chain and belt meshes are tested
  tangent to both wheels in the site, in their planes.
- **Animation as in the screw world** (`SiteDrive.update`): each wheel at `angle × ratio`, the same guard against the wagon-wheel illusion (`BLUR_FROM`, `BLUR_FULL`, `BAND_PERIODS_MAX`: a test ties them
  to `screw/scene.ts`), the chain and belt patterns at the rim speed, the discs hidden when the part is ghosted or hidden. After a redraw the wheels start from the screw's angle, so the large sprocket
  stays in step with the shaft. `createSiteScene({ ratios })` takes the live ratios as `createScrewScene` does (`lab.ts` passes `sim.params.chainRatio` and `.beltRatio`); a ratio that moves by 0.04
  redraws only the small sprocket, the small pulley, the chain and the belt (`refreshDrive` re-registers their meshes in the part), the screw and the masonry stay.
- **What the true offsets do at the design point.** The shaft crosses the chain plane at x = -7.72 m, 0.47 m over the datum (0.56 m over the water). The countershaft is 0.78 m to the south of it, that is
  at z = -4.82 m, **over the south strip of the canal** (0.67 m in from the wall); the generator is 1.62 m to the south, at z = -3.98 m, on the south wall and the bank. The screw world's platform
  cannot stand there, so the machine is not moved: **the support is adapted**, in the site frame (`SITE_DRIVE`, estimates, and heights read from the layout):
  - a **beam** across the end of the canal (0.34 × 0.11 m, wall to wall) carries the upper bearing on a plinth of masonry; its north end is let into the north wall, its south end rests on a **pier on the
    quay**, at the east edge of the pit (the bearing stands over x = -8.14 m, where the pit's south wall is only at the quay's level);
  - a **shelf** leaves the south wall over the water (0.14 m thick, on a root 0.22 m deep against the wall, 0.88 m out) and carries the countershaft's two pillow blocks on the wedges of the screw
    world; its top is 0.1 m under the lowest point of the belt (+0.31 m);
  - the generator stands on the south wall and the bank, on its own wedge.
    Nothing is in the water: the highest water the world draws at the end of the canal is the full bucket's (-0.098 m, `inletLevel(l, 1)`), every underside is at least 0.1 m over it (the beam 0.11, the shelf
    0.26), the pier is south of the inner face of the south wall, and the shelf leaves 2 m of the canal open (a test sweeps the states that move the water). A bigger screw lifts the drive over the walls
    (a pier for the north end of the beam, one for the generator on the bank) and a smaller one puts it over the basin: the masonry follows from the layout and still stands on something (tested from a 0.5 m
    screw to a 2.5 m one).
- **Budget.** The drive adds 32 draw calls and 8,500 triangles (26 and 8,000 for the machine, which is merged by part as in the screw world, 6 for the masonry and the bearing); the whole site is 163 draw
  calls and 165,500 triangles (limits 220 and 400,000); building the drive takes about 5 ms, the site about 255 ms in Chrome (it was 240 to 250); a frame writes the rotation of 6 groups and two offsets.
- **Weakest.** The bank and the top of the south wall are at 0.55 m and the base of the generator's feet is at 0.49 to 0.59 m: the west foot, the small pulley (3 cm) and the lower run of the belt (it enters
  the south wall about 5 cm under its top) are slightly in the ground; a notch in the wall and the bank would clear them (the land is the scenery's, not touched here). In the exploded view the
  cable stays where it was (it is scenery) while the chamber rises, as the old one did.

## Completion wave A: the `generator` and the `powerroom` worlds (`src/three/generator`, `src/three/powerroom`)

The lab standard asks for four worlds: the two new ones are built from `GENERATOR_DESIGN` and `POWERROOM` at the end of `dims.ts` (every number an `estimate` of ours, tagged; the lead's
brief calls the first block `GENERATOR`, a name the outside of the blue machine already has, so the inside is `GENERATOR_DESIGN`). What the simulation gives (600 rpm, 39 N·m, 2.2 kW, 53.7 V,

### The `generator` world

The permanent-magnet generator of the `screw` and `site` worlds, opened: a three-phase **radial-flux machine with an inner rotor and surface magnets**. **16 poles, 24 slots** (estimates: the
sources give none), so `f = poles × rpm / 120` = **80 Hz** at 600 rpm (`electricalFrequency`); 24 teeth with **one coil on each** (phases A, B, C follow each other round the stator: slots per pole
and phase = 1/2, the teeth are 120 electrical degrees apart), 16 magnets north and south alternating, **air gap 1.5 mm**, rotor and stack 150 mm, two 62 mm ball bearings, a frame with two end bells,
a terminal box with a block of three studs. Frame of the world: X along the shaft (the stub, where the small pulley sits, at x < 0), the front face of the body at x = 0, Y up, Z towards the viewer.

### The `powerroom` world

## What is estimated, what is simplified

- **Estimates** (all tagged in `dims.ts`): every size that is not a result of the simulation: the trough (7 cm wall, 0.3 R sides), the piers, the bearings, the platform and the pools, the chain (ISO 16B, 25.4 mm, 56 teeth),
  the pulleys (0.40 m), the countershaft (44 mm), the generator (R 0.155 m), the electronics, the weir, the notch, the fish pass, the canal, the rack (8 cm spacing), the gate, the chamber, the buildings. The hardware of a
  screw of another size is scaled by `(D / 0.86)^0.7`, between 0.6 and 2.2 (purely visual). **Handedness** (right) and the number of modules (a quarter turn each) are choices: the sources show neither.
- **Simplified**: the helicoid blade is a thick ruled slab (5 steps per quarter turn, the tip a polygon); the water pockets ignore the 4 mm gap and keep the blade thickness out of the volume, so the section's full bucket is a little
  less than the simulation's volume (checked within 10 %); the pocket's outer wall is the blade tip; the pool and basin are boxes (the basin's polygon is exact in the section); the screw in the site is the same geometry at 0.7 detail (its drive train is the screw world's own machine, see below); the weir is a wall with a sheet of water, not a nappe; the chain is a band with a dashed pattern (no links, no pins), the belt a flat band (no V); sprocket teeth are trapezoids; the rack and the gate have no hinges; the
  river has no waves, only streaks; trees are icospheres. The platform is flat (see above); the generator's rotor is not drawn; the electronics are boxes with terminals and cables, not circuits.
- **Not drawn**: guards over the chain and the belt (a real machine needs them, the lab's text says so), the ladder and the hoist of the installation, ice, sediment, the fish ladder's baffles, the real stage-discharge of the river.
